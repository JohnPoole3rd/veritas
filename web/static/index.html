Chapter 01

Two minds that never met.

Every dot below is a real satellite tile, placed by what DINOv2 thinks of it. Every star is a real sentence, placed by MiniLM. Scroll to apply the rotation.

0.00mean cosine, tile ↔ its own caption
Before

Two separate spaces. DINOv2 sorted 142 million images; MiniLM sorted a billion sentence pairs. Neither has ever seen the other. Measured against its own caption, a tile scores ~0, no better than chance.

The rotation

We take labelled EuroSAT image–caption pairs and ask SVD for the one orthogonal matrix W that best lines them up. It can turn the space and flip it, but never stretch or bend it. Nothing inside either model changes.

After

Rivers drift toward the word “river”; forests toward “forest”. The mean cosine rises to 0.29. The geometry was already shared. The rotation only had to find it.

2-D PCA projection of real 384-D vectors: 360 held-out EuroSAT tiles and 50 captions.

Chapter 04

Where shade is missing.

Search finds places. For a climatologist, the real question is what those places do. Next to the model we put two measurements: greenness from Sentinel-2 and surface temperature from Landsat. Scroll through one summer morning in Upper Arlington.

True colour · Sentinel-2
01 · What the satellite saw

The same 1 Aug 2025 Sentinel-2 pass, at 10 m. Canopy, cul-de-sacs, the Scioto River on the west edge, and the big parking lots of the commercial strips.

02 · Greenness (measured)

NDVI = (near-infrared − red) / (near-infrared + red). Leaves reflect near-infrared strongly, so dense canopy glows dark green and roofs and asphalt fall toward tan.

03 · Heat (measured)

Landsat 8's thermal band, 9 days earlier: the skin temperature of roofs, roads and leaves at the late-morning overpass. It is not air temperature, but it marks where heat builds up.

Each dot is one 640 m square of land. The greener the square, the cooler it runs. Across 155 land squares, the least-green third is ~5 °C hotter than the greenest third.

    How a run works

    1. The clearest summer Sentinel-2 L2A pass is read straight from the public archive (Element84 Earth Search) and cut into 640 m squares. 2. Frozen DINOv2 embeds each square; the one rotation maps it next to the 50 captions, and the best match is its land-cover estimate. 3. NDVI comes from the same pass. Surface temperature comes from the nearest clear Landsat 8/9 Collection 2 pass (Microsoft Planetary Computer). 4. Simple statistics: OLS slope, Pearson r, tercile contrast, and a hot-and-bare ranking.

    Chapter 06

    What it gets wrong.

    A tool you can trust tells you where not to trust it. These come from our own logged runs.

    Abstract questions

    “climate change”

    Climate change is not a land cover, so there is nothing to match, yet the tool still returns tiles. It cannot say “I don't know”, and a low score is not a reliable warning.

    Read before you use it

    Known limits

    • Fit on European tiles (EuroSAT). In Ohio it barely beats always answering “Residential”.
    • Thin rivers look like roads at 10 m: River → Highway is the top EuroSAT mistake (33% of river tiles).
    • Ten classes only. No “solar farm”, “wetland”, or “tree species”.
    • Surface temperature is skin temperature at one late-morning overpass, not air temperature or a heat-health index.
    • The NDVI–temperature slope is a description of one scene, not a causal effect of planting.
    Method

    Five steps, no training.

    01 · SeeDINOv2 ViT-S/14Frozen. Each 64×64 Sentinel-2 chip becomes 384 numbers.
    02 · Readall-MiniLM-L6-v2Frozen. Each sentence becomes 384 numbers.
    03 · AlignProcrustesW = UVᵀ from the SVD of XᵀY on labelled EuroSAT training pairs.
    04 · AskCosine searchQuery text ↔ rotated tiles; label = best-matching caption.
    05 · MeasureNDVI + LSTSentinel-2 bands and Landsat thermal, never from the model.

    W* = argmin ‖XW − Y‖ subject to WᵀW = I  W = U Vᵀ, where U Σ Vᵀ = svd(XᵀY)
